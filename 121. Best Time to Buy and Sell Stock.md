# 121. Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock
LeetCode: https://leetcode.com/problems/best-time-to-buy-and-sell-stock/ · Easy · Pattern: Two pointers / sliding window

## Problem
`prices[i]` is the price of a stock on day `i`. You may **buy on one day** and **sell on a later day**. Return the biggest profit you can make, or `0` if you can't make any.

```
Input:  prices = [10, 1, 5, 6, 7, 1]     Output: 6     (buy at 1, sell at 7)
Input:  prices = [10, 8, 7, 5, 2]         Output: 0     (prices only fall)
```

## Words you need first
| Word | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **profit** | Sell price minus buy price. |
| **`l` (left pointer)** | The day we'd **buy**. |
| **`r` (right pointer)** | The day we'd **sell**. It's always later than `l`. |

## How to come up with it (the thinking)
**Step 1: the obvious idea.** Try every buy day with every later sell day and keep the best. About n × n pairs. Slow.

**Step 2: what do we really want?** To sell on day `r` for the most profit, we'd want to have bought on the **cheapest** day before it.

**Step 3: the trick.** Walk `r` forward one day at a time and keep `l` on the cheapest day seen so far.
- If today's price is **higher** than the buy price → we could sell today. Work out the profit, and keep the best.
- If today's price is **lower** than (or equal to) the buy price → today is a better day to buy. Move `l` to today.

## Solution
```python
class Solution:
    def maxProfit(self, prices: List[int]) -> int:
        l, r = 0, 1
        maxP = 0
        while r < len(prices):
            if prices[l] < prices[r]:
                profit = prices[r] - prices[l]
                maxP = max(maxP, profit)
            else:
                l = r
            r += 1
        return maxP
```

## Line by line
| Line | What it does |
|---|---|
| `l, r = 0, 1` | Buy on day 0, and consider selling on day 1. |
| `maxP = 0` | Best profit so far. Zero means "no profit possible". |
| `while r < len(prices):` | Keep going until the sell day passes the last day. |
| `if prices[l] < prices[r]:` | Is selling today for more than the buy price? |
| `profit = prices[r] - prices[l]` | Yes → this is the profit. |
| `maxP = max(maxP, profit)` | Keep the bigger of old best and this profit. |
| `else: l = r` | No → today is cheaper (or equal), so it's a better day to buy. |
| `r += 1` | Move on to the next possible sell day. |

## Watch it run
`prices = [10, 1, 5, 6, 7, 1]`

| `l` | `r` | `prices[l]` (buy) | `prices[r]` (sell) | What happens | `maxP` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 10 | 1 | 1 is cheaper → buy here instead, `l = 1` | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | 1 | 5 | profit 4 | 4 |
| 1 | 3 | 1 | 6 | profit 5 | 5 |
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 7 | profit 6 | 6 |
| 1 | 5 | 1 | 1 | not higher → `l = 5` | 6 |

`r` goes past the end, so we stop. Answer: **6** ✅

## How fast is it?
- **Time: O(n).** `r` visits each day once.
- **Space: O(1).** Just a few variables.
