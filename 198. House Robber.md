# 198. House Robber
LeetCode: https://leetcode.com/problems/house-robber/ · Medium · Pattern: Dynamic programming

## Problem
`nums[i]` is the money in house `i`, along a street. You **can't rob two houses next to each other** (the alarms would go off). What's the **most money** you can rob?

```
Input: nums = [1, 1, 3, 3]        Output: 4     (rob house 0 and house 2, or 3:  1 + 3)
Input: nums = [2, 9, 8, 3, 6]     Output: 16    (2 + 8 + 6)
```

## Words you need first
| Word | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **adjacent** | Next to each other. |
| **dynamic programming (DP)** | Build the answer step by step from smaller answers, remembering them. |
| **`rob1`, `rob2`** | `rob2` = the best total using houses up to the **previous** house. `rob1` = the best total up to the house **before that**. |

## How to come up with it (the thinking)
**Step 1: greedy fails.** "Always rob the biggest house" or "rob every other house" can go wrong. For `[2, 9, 8, 3, 6]`, robbing every other house from the start (2 + 8 + 6 = 16) is best, but for `[1, 9, 1, 1, 9]`, it isn't. We need to consider choices carefully.

**Step 2: at each house there are exactly two choices.**
- **Rob it:** you get its money, but the previous house is off-limits. So the total is `this house + best up to two houses back`.
- **Skip it:** the total is just the best up to the **previous** house.

Take whichever is bigger.

**Step 3: we only need the last two answers.** So keep two variables and slide them forward house by house.

## Solution
```python
class Solution:
    def rob(self, nums: List[int]) -> int:
        rob1, rob2 = 0, 0

        for n in nums:
            temp = max(n + rob1, rob2)
            rob1 = rob2
            rob2 = temp
        return rob2
```

## Line by line
| Line | What it does |
|---|---|
| `rob1, rob2 = 0, 0` | Before any house, the best is 0. |
| `for n in nums:` | Go house by house; `n` is this house's money. |
| `temp = max(n + rob1, rob2)` | The best total up to this house: **rob it** (`n + rob1`) or **skip it** (`rob2`), whichever is bigger. |
| `rob1 = rob2` | Slide forward: the old "previous" becomes "two back". |
| `rob2 = temp` | The new best becomes "the previous". |
| `return rob2` | After the last house, `rob2` is the best total for the whole street. |

## Watch it run
`nums = [2, 9, 8, 3, 6]`

| House | `n` | `rob1` (2 back) | `rob2` (prev best) | Rob it: `n + rob1` | Skip it: `rob2` | New best | `rob1` → | `rob2` → |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 0 | **2** | 0 | 2 |
| 1 | 9 | 0 | 2 | 9 | 2 | **9** | 2 | 9 |
| 2 | 8 | 2 | 9 | 10 | 9 | **10** | 9 | 10 |
| 3 | 3 | 9 | 10 | 12 | 10 | **12** | 10 | 12 |
| 4 | 6 | 10 | 12 | 16 | 12 | **16** | 12 | 16 |

Answer: **16** ✅

## How fast is it?
- **Time: O(n).** One pass.
- **Space: O(1).** Two variables.
